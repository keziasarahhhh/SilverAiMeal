The problem

Institutional kitchens aren't personal calorie trackers

They manage bulk and donated food for dozens of seniors a day, often with few staff and volunteer cooks.

Food safety & expiry risk

Large purchased and donated deliveries make manual First-In, First-Out tracking fail — and expired food is dangerous for frail seniors.

Nutrition & texture gaps

Cooking "whatever arrived" (often starchy donations) leads to low-protein meals and sarcopenia risk, and soft-texture needs get overlooked.

No dish-level leftover data

All food waste is weighed together, so nobody knows which dish went uneaten, what it cost, or what seniors actually like.

The solution

One closed loop, three simple modules

1

Quick-Snap Expiration Tracker

Photograph a food label; OCR reads the expiry date (including 民國 dates) and sorts stock into Red (today), Yellow (within 3 days) and Green (safe).

Prevents foodborne illness and enforces FIFO without manual logs.

2

AI Senior Meal Planner

Tick the red/yellow items and get a group menu that uses them first, with IDDSI texture guidance (minced, thickened…) and a protein-gap alert with what to add.

Helps volunteer cooks serve safe, balanced, high-protein meals.

3

Leftover & Preference Slider

After the meal, slide 0% – 75%+ per dish. The app calculates waste cost and updates dish ratings that refine the next menu.

Tracks waste cost and adapts menus to seniors' tastes — no scales needed.

How it works

A daily routine that takes minutes

  1. 01

    Snap on arrival

    When deliveries or donations arrive, staff photograph each label. Stock appears on the traffic-light board, soonest first.

  2. 02

    Plan before cooking

    Pick today's expiring items, the number of seniors and their texture level. The planner suggests dishes, quantities and prep steps.

  3. 03

    Slide after serving

    Record leftovers per dish with one slider. Costs and ratings update automatically and shape tomorrow's menu.
